Learning outcome 01 / History

Explain key moments in the historical development of AI and ML and relate them to modern AI systems.

My evidence

1956 Dartmouth Workshop

John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester, and Claude Shannon proposed a summer research project at Dartmouth to investigate whether machines could perform tasks that people associate with intelligence. Their 1955 proposal used the term artificial intelligence and outlined questions about how computers might use language, form concepts, solve problems, and improve through learning. Researchers gathered at Dartmouth in the summer of 1956 to explore these ideas.

Impact on the field. The workshop helped establish AI as a distinct area of research by giving people from different disciplines a shared name and a set of problems to work on. One idea I connect to this event is that an intelligent behavior can be broken down into something a computer can represent and evaluate. In our course, search makes that idea concrete. An agent can represent a problem as states and actions, then explore possible steps until it reaches a goal.

2012 AlexNet ImageNet Breakthrough

Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton used graphics processing units (GPUs) to train deep convolutional neural networks on labeled images. Their winning entry in the 2012 ImageNet competition achieved a top-five error rate of 15.3%, compared with 26.2% for the runner-up. A top-five error occurs when the correct category is missing from the model’s five highest-ranked guesses. If the correct category appears fourth, for example, the prediction still counts as correct under that metric.

Impact on the field. AlexNet showed how effectively deep networks could learn visual features from large datasets, helping make deep learning a leading approach in computer vision. I see why that matters in my basketball trainer project. Color alone can be a weak clue when the ball and court look similar. A trained detector can combine several cues, such as shape, edges, and texture, to help recognize the ball under different lighting conditions.

Further reading · Original AlexNet paper (2012)



2017 Introduction of the Transformer

Ashish Vaswani and his colleagues introduced the Transformer and evaluated it on machine translation. Its self-attention mechanism lets the model weigh relationships between tokens, which can be words or parts of words. For example, an earlier sentence about a circuit can help a model interpret the word “current” later in a passage. The architecture also allowed more of the training computation to happen in parallel.

Impact on the field. The Transformer became the basis for many large language models. ChatGPT’s public release in 2022 showed how people could use such a model through a conversation that included follow-up questions. I use that interaction pattern in Jay, my Raspberry Pi AI companion that has a chat dashboard. If I ask, “What time was my demo again?” Jay can pass a relevant stored reminder to the model as context. The reminder supplies the time, and the model turns that information into a conversational response without needing to be retrained.

Further reading · Attention Is All You Need (2017)
