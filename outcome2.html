Learning outcome 02 / Ethics

Identify and discuss ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications.

My evidence



Amazon AI Hiring

One AI ethics case that really stood out to me was Amazon's AI hiring algorithm that showed bias against women applying for technical positions. Amazon created this system to make the hiring process easier by reviewing resumes and ranking applicants based on their qualifications. However, the AI was trained on past resumes that mostly came from men, which caused it to favor male applicants and penalize certain things associated with women. Amazon eventually stopped using the project because it couldn't guarantee that the system would be fair. I personally connect with this case because, as a Computer Engineering student, I'm currently going through the process of applying for internships and co-ops. I've spent countless hours working on my resume, building projects like my Jay AI assistant, learning new skills, and gaining experience from my internship at NBME. Even with all that experience, getting an interview can still be difficult. So imagine putting in all that work, only to get rejected before someone even has the chance to look at what you can actually do. It makes me question how many qualified people could be overlooked because an AI system learned the wrong patterns from its training data. Especially when applying for engineering positions, I believe your skills, experience, and willingness to learn should matter more than anything else. Something that bothers me is that applicants might never know whether they were rejected because they weren't qualified or because an algorithm unfairly ranked them. This could affect people's careers, financial opportunities, and confidence in applying for certain positions. It could also make it harder for people from underrepresented backgrounds to enter the technology industry. I also find this case interesting because I'm on both sides of the situation in a way. I'm someone applying for jobs where AI might be involved in screening my application, but I'm also studying to become an engineer who could eventually build systems like these. Through developing Jay, I've learned that creating an AI system involves more than just making it work. You have to think about how it processes information, where that information comes from, and how its decisions affect people. If I were developing a system like Amazon's, I would want to make sure it was tested for bias and that recruiters weren't relying entirely on its recommendations. To me, AI should be a tool that helps people recognize opportunities and potential, not something that prevents a qualified person from getting a fair chance.
