Learning outcome 02 / Ethics

Identify and discuss ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications.

My evidence



Auto Insurance

In the insurance industry, many companies are starting to use artificial intelligence to help calculate insurance rates and make decisions based on customer information. SureDrive Insurance is one of these companies that introduced an AI system called PriceRight to calculate insurance premiums based on driving history, zip codes, and other personal information. The purpose of this system is to determine how much a customer should pay based on their driving risk. However, an investigation discovered that people living in certain zip codes were being charged higher premiums even though they had the same driving records, accident histories, and vehicles as other customers. The company argued that their AI system did not use race or income when calculating insurance rates. Although this was true, other information such as zip codes, phone types, and driving schedules could still indirectly relate to a person's race or income. Another concern was that the company was already aware of these problems but continued telling customers that the AI was free from bias. Certain employees were also changing customer information to provide lower rates for people they favored. First of all, one of the biggest ethical issues associated with PriceRight is algorithmic bias and how it can affect people even when the system is not directly using race or income. I believe that just because an AI system is making decisions based on data does not necessarily mean that those decisions are fair. The information being used can still contain patterns that create unfair outcomes for certain groups of people. This clearly shows how an AI system designed to improve efficiency can also create problems when the company does not properly evaluate the results. Additionally, SureDrive's decision to ignore these issues raises concerns about transparency and accountability because customers are being told that the system is unbiased when the company knows otherwise. This could have a greater impact on lower-income communities because they may have to pay higher insurance premiums despite having similar driving records to others. I believe SureDrive should regularly evaluate its AI system for bias, explain how insurance rates are determined, and prevent employees from changing customer information unfairly. Overall, this case demonstrates how companies need to consider the ethical consequences of AI rather than only focusing on how efficiently the system performs.



Easy Scorer

Education is one of the many fields where artificial intelligence is becoming more involved in helping teachers and students with their academic work. Springfield School District introduced an AI system called ScoreWrite that was designed to grade student essays and provide feedback based on their writing. The purpose of this system was to make grading more consistent because different teachers may give different scores for the same essay. At first, this seems like a good idea because it could help teachers save time while providing students with the same grading standards. However, researchers discovered that students who were learning English or using different dialects were receiving lower scores even though their essays had strong ideas and organization. The main reason was that the AI had been trained mostly on essays written in standard academic English, which caused it to favor a particular writing style rather than the actual quality of the student's work. Furthermore, teachers began relying more on the AI instead of providing their own detailed feedback, while the school district started using these scores to evaluate teacher performance even though the system was not designed for that purpose. One of the biggest concerns I have with this situation is how the training data used in AI can affect the decisions it makes toward different groups of students. Although ScoreWrite was created to make grading more consistent, that does not necessarily mean the scores are accurate or fair for everyone. Students have different ways of expressing their ideas, and I believe their writing should be evaluated based on their understanding of the material rather than only following one particular writing style. This clearly shows how algorithmic bias can affect students academically because they may receive grades that do not properly represent their abilities. Additionally, teachers becoming too dependent on AI could remove an important part of education, which is providing students with feedback and helping them understand their mistakes. This could also affect teachers if their performance is being evaluated through scores that may already contain bias. I believe AI should be used as a tool to support teachers rather than completely replacing their judgment. Schools should include more diverse writing examples when training these systems and have teachers review the results before making final decisions. Overall, this case shows how AI can provide benefits in education, but without proper human oversight, it could create more problems for the students and teachers who depend on it.
