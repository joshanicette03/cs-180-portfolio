Learning outcome 02 / Ethics

Identify and discuss ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications.

My evidence

  • Auto Insurance

    In the insurance industry, many companies are starting to use artificial intelligence to help calculate insurance rates and make decisions based on customer information. SureDrive Insurance is one of these companies that introduced an AI system called PriceRight to calculate insurance premiums based on driving history, zip codes, and other personal information. The purpose of this system is to determine how much a customer should pay based on their driving risk. However, an investigation discovered that people living in certain zip codes were being charged higher premiums even though they had the same driving records, accident histories, and vehicles as other customers. The company argued that their AI system did not use race or income when calculating insurance rates. Although this was true, other information such as zip codes, phone types, and driving schedules could still indirectly relate to a person's race or income. Another concern was that the company was already aware of these problems but continued telling customers that the AI was free from bias. Certain employees were also changing customer information to provide lower rates for people they favored. First of all, one of the biggest ethical issues associated with PriceRight is algorithmic bias and how it can affect people even when the system is not directly using race or income. I believe that just because an AI system is making decisions based on data does not necessarily mean that those decisions are fair. The information being used can still contain patterns that create unfair outcomes for certain groups of people. This clearly shows how an AI system designed to improve efficiency can also create problems when the company does not properly evaluate the results. Additionally, SureDrive's decision to ignore these issues raises concerns about transparency and accountability because customers are being told that the system is unbiased when the company knows otherwise. This could have a greater impact on lower-income communities because they may have to pay higher insurance premiums despite having similar driving records to others. I believe SureDrive should regularly evaluate its AI system for bias, explain how insurance rates are determined, and prevent employees from changing customer information unfairly. Overall, this case demonstrates how companies need to consider the ethical consequences of AI rather than only focusing on how efficiently the system performs.
